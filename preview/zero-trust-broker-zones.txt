Zero Trust Access Broker: What Each Zone
Does
Graduation project · 14 October 2026
The broker checks every login, web request and SSH connection against identity, device health
and risk, and can cut access mid-session. The architecture follows NIST SP 800-207 and is split
into five zones.

6. How the zones work together
Three stories to trace on the diagram, each crossing every zone.
Web login
 1.  The passkey signs the login and the employee signs in at the login portal.
2.  The web proxy asks the Policy Engine.
3.  The engine checks the risk score and device health, then allows.
4.  Traffic reaches the internal web app.
5.  The decision is written to the audit chain.
SSH to a server
 1.  The employee types ssh server.
2.  The SSH helper sends the SPA knock and requests a certificate.
3.  The SSH CA asks the Policy Engine; production may need admin approval from the
dashboard.
4.  The CA signs a 30-minute certificate and the employee connects.
5.  The certificate expires on its own. Replaying it in the attack demo fails.


4
Attack mid-session
 1.  The posture agent reports disk encryption was turned off, and the ML flags unusual
downloads.
2.  The risk score drops below the threshold.
3.  The Policy Administrator kills the session at the proxy.
4.  The employee is notified and the admin gets an alert.
5.  Every step is recorded in the audit chain.
