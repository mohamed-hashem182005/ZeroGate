07FLOWS / APPROVAL

When the score is
a question, not a no.

A request in the 40–59 band is not denied; it is suspended and sent to a human. The approver sees the score, the reasons, the device posture, and the requested resource. A grant is single-use, time-boxed, and re-checked against policy before it is issued.

FIG. 075 lifelines9 messagesSLA 15 minutes

Why a human in the loop

The approval band exists because some context is genuinely ambiguous. A contractor asking for a production wiki at 21:30 from a new ASN is not obviously an attacker, and it is not obviously safe. A score in the 50s is the system saying “I have reasons to hesitate.” A human with the context can resolve the ambiguity faster and more accurately than a threshold can.

The important design choice is that the human does not get a blank check. The approval is bounded by the requested TTL, capped by policy, single-use, and re-checked at resolution. The approver grants a task window, not a role. The engine still owns the decision; the human supplies one missing input.

The rules behind the queue

Every rule in the table below is enforced server-side. The UI mirrors them, but the UI is not the control. A request that expires while the approver is reading it cannot be approved. A request whose policy changed during the wait is re-evaluated. A grant that was already consumed cannot be replayed.

What the approver sees

Enough context to say yes or no

  • Requester, team, and role.
  • Resource, sensitivity, and requested action.
  • Trust score, band, and the top three reason codes.
  • Device posture and the last posture change.
  • Requested TTL and the policy ceiling.
  • Prior access to the same resource, if any.
What the approver does not get

No silent expansion

  • No ability to approve their own request.
  • No ability to exceed the policy TTL ceiling.
  • No ability to grant a different resource.
  • No ability to reuse a consumed grant.
  • No ability to suppress the audit event.

Failure and edge cases

  • Two approvers race: the first decision wins; the second receives 409 CONFLICT and the current state.
  • Approver is the requester: the API rejects with 403 SELF_APPROVAL, even if the UI is bypassed.
  • Request expires during approval: the resolution step fails closed and the request moves to EXPIRED.
  • Policy changes during the wait: the engine re-evaluates at resolution; a new deny wins.
  • Grant is never consumed: it expires after 30 minutes; a sweeper records the expiry.
  • Audit append fails: the grant is not issued; the request remains pending with an error state.
Priority note

JIT is an add-on in the project scope. The core system must work without it: an approval-band request can be denied by policy when the queue is not deployed. The design keeps the state machine and API ready so the add-on does not change the decision contract.
